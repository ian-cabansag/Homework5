What I would simplify

Where one capable tool-using agent is enough

These are small, narrow records. For ticket 102, one agent could read the lease/date/cash, quote the $2,400 payment, and stop for approval; Facilities and Accounting mostly re-read one shared context. For ticket 103, one agent could read the 8-versus-20 stock and $22/$58 price, then write the internal draft. Ticket 101 could also be one agent for the stock, invoice, vendor block, quote, and draft—the Inventory specialist did add useful scrutiny, but the duplicated reads outweighed the extra perspective.

I would keep the five-role setup for genuinely different evidence or a second opinion on a consequential decision. I would not make a specialist delegation mandatory just because the role exists.

What this team can and cannot do today

Three realistic problems it can handle

  1. Check and pay recorded obligations with operator approval. The ticket 101/102 context and get_payment_quote showed $840 + $2,400 = $3,240, leaving $160 from $3,400. Ian explicitly approved both; the API/MCP route executed each and rechecked cash. The system still cannot reserve future expenses.
  2. Check a vendor’s recorded lead time and shipment block. list_vendors and get_vendor_shipping_eligibility report Bulldog Print Co’s five-day lead and open-invoice rule. After invoice 501 was paid, a real follow-up read showed no open invoices and shipping eligibility; the system still cannot contact the vendor or confirm delivery.
  3. Prepare a grounded partial-availability response. The ticket 103 stock/pricing tool can support a draft that says 8 M hoodies are recorded at a $58 list price, with no approved discount. Customer Service can keep that text internal and avoid accepting the 20-unit request.

Three problems it cannot complete with today’s tools

  1. Source and buy the 12 missing hoodies. There is no hoodie-specific vendor match (vendors list apparel reprints, mugs/small goods, and courier service), purchase-order table, or PO/payment quote. I’d need a vendor–SKU catalog, purchasing records, a procurement capability, and an MCP order/approval flow that rechecks cash and vendor eligibility.
  2. Reserve, ship, or receive the tee/hoodie units. The current inventory tool is read-only and there is no order-fulfillment, reservation, shipment, or receiving record. I’d need those tables and guarded MCP writes, plus a human/vendor-confirmed shipment status; a five-day lead field alone is not proof of fulfillment.
  3. Choose and authorize a bulk discount. The pricing table has only unit cost and list price ($22/$58 for the hoodie), not discount tiers or approval policy. I’d need a documented pricing policy, an authorized override workflow/tool, and a pricing approver capability. Until then, the agent must not invent a discount.
